#+title: Landscape regional identity and visual references
#+startup: overview

* Regional-identity revision — 2026-11-03
The earlier collection leaned too heavily on a shared parchment background and blue keywords. This revision gives each region a distinct background family, related light/dark surfaces, and region-specific keyword/accent hues. Provence takes the stronger purple emphasis of Catppuccin Latte as a readability/style reference, while using its own lavender colors.

These are original artistic interpretations of recognizable regional scenery and materials, not official regional palettes. Reference photographs were actually viewed; colors were designed rather than sampled or claimed as exact photographic matches. Links record the research without redistributing the photographs. Temporary downloads and Emacs screenshots are removed after review.

* Design directions
| Region   | Visual vocabulary                                     | Light / dark backgrounds               | Keyword family |
|----------+-------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+----------------|
| Provence | Lavender, limestone, olive foliage, ochre             | Lavender paper / aubergine dusk        | Purple         |
| Roma     | Travertine, warm masonry, red, bronze                 | Travertine cream / wine-dark stone     | Carmine        |
| Sicilia  | Majolica cobalt, turquoise, citrus gold, terracotta   | Glazed ivory / sea-and-basalt navy     | Cobalt         |
| Umbria   | Olive, forest green, stone, harvest gold              | Sage paper / forest shade              | Forest green   |
| Venezia  | Lagoon teal, weathered plaster, rose, gold            | Lagoon pearl / deep petrol             | Teal           |
| Iceland  | Basalt, cold water, moss, ice, volcanic warmth        | Glacial blue paper / blue-black basalt | Lichen green   |
| Firenze  | White/green/pink marble, terracotta, Renaissance blue | Rosy marble / green marble shadow      | Terracotta     |
| Bologna  | Brick red, ochre arcades, warm plaster, ink           | Terracotta plaster / brick shadow      | Brick red      |
| Napoli   | Naples yellow, tuff, bay blue, coral                  | Naples-yellow paper / bay at night     | Gold ochre     |

The pairings are intentionally interpretive: a night palette is a designed counterpart, not proof that a region has one characteristic night color. Quiet body text retains a lightly tinted near-neutral color; syntax uses clearer accents with enough separation for code. Selection, current-line, secondary surfaces and borders follow each region's base and accent rather than borrowing the old common brown ramp.

* Readability and consistency
- Core light syntax keeps at least 6.5:1 contrast on base, alternate and current-line surfaces; dark syntax targets at least 5.5:1.
- Body text remains at least 7:1, and comments, diagnostics and highlight pairs at least 4.8:1 in the existing tests.
- The five primary light syntax roles retain a minimum CIELAB Delta E76 of 30. Some supporting hues were shifted slightly to separate blue/violet or red/ochre; this is a broad regression guard, not a color-vision accessibility guarantee.
- Light keywords remain bold; functions and types retain normal weight. No font sizes, fonts, Org view settings, language servers or keybindings change.
- Selectable names, flat directory structure and the installed Doom theme framework remain unchanged. Other custom families are unchanged.

Implementation: [[file:landscape-palettes.el][palette data]]. Validation: [[file:LANDSCAPE-VALIDATION.org][test report]].

* Iceland moss follow-up
Iceland keeps its glacial light base and blue-black basalt dark base, but lichen
green now owns keywords, cursor/link accents, selections, current-line and
secondary surfaces, and added/success colors. Cyan remains on strings and
blue on functions. This restores moss as a dominant identity rather than
confining it to occasional strings. On the light variant, moss text is deeper
for readability while pale moss-green surfaces provide the light-green color.

* Stronger signature-color pass
The other eight regions now receive the same deliberate exaggeration of their
recognizable colors as Iceland. Background tints are stronger, and selections,
current-line surfaces, borders and the frequent syntax roles carry the regional
identity. Comments retain a quiet tint so they do not compete with keywords.

| Region   | Stronger signature                                                                 |
|----------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Provence | Richer lavender paper, deeper violet dusk, purple highlights and olive/gold syntax |
| Roma     | Warmer travertine, wine-colored shadows, carmine keywords and bronze functions     |
| Sicilia  | Lemon-ivory ceramic light base, cobalt night, turquoise and citrus accents         |
| Umbria   | More olive-tinted paper, deep forest shade, harvest gold and berry/earth accents   |
| Venezia  | More saturated lagoon paper and petrol night, mint-teal and rose plaster           |
| Firenze  | Rosier marble paper, stronger green-marble night, terracotta and malachite syntax  |
| Bologna  | Stronger terracotta plaster and brick shadow, brick-red keywords and ochre gold    |
| Napoli   | More pronounced Naples-yellow paper, richer bay-blue night and gold accents        |

Iceland is preserved exactly from the moss restoration. Other custom theme
families, names and font weights remain unchanged. This is a more expressive
interpretation of the same researched materials and imagery, not a new claim
of exact geographic or photographic color matching.

* Provence warmth and sage refinement — 2026-11-05
The Provence light surfaces retain a visible lavender cast with a little more
warmth: base =#ECE1F0=, secondary =#E4D5E8=, current line =#DFCEE5= and selection
=#D0B7E0=. The keyword/accent violet =#58298D= is less saturated than the earlier
=#5600B8= while preserving the established light-syntax contrast target. Search
focus follows the same accent. Dark modules use a dustier sage =#9DC8AD= instead
of mint =#82D1A3=. The dark aubergine base, olive functions and ochre/gold strings
remain unchanged.

This is a restrained interpretation of the lavender, olive foliage, warm stone
and ochre vocabulary visible in the references, not a sampled photographic
palette. The public [[https://magazin.aktualne.cz/cestovani/levandulova-pole-v-provence-fotogalerie/r~9c5a9dec20a011ee8980ac1f6b220ee8/][landscape photograph]] was viewed alongside the palette;
[[https://provence-alpes-cotedazur.com/en/things-to-do/agritourism/the-crops-which-shape-the-landscapes-of-the-south-of-france/][regional tourism material]] documents lavender and silvery olive foliage.
No other regional palette was changed.

** Function/module separation follow-up
Function olive shifts slightly toward yellow: =#334B00= in Provence light and
=#CDD47E= in Provence dark. Module sage remains =#226145=/=#9DC8AD= and bold.
This increases the color distinction within module/function chains while
retaining the olive/sage relationship. Type, parameter and string colors are
unchanged. The light function color retains the 6.5:1 syntax contrast target
and the existing minimum separation from other primary syntax roles.
